THE ROLE

What a Scrum Master does

Coach

Coaches the team

Helps the team see how it works, question its own habits, and get better at solving its own problems.

Enable

Makes sure the events happen

Ensures planning, reviews and retros take place and stay useful; the team runs them, not the Scrum Master. Steps in to facilitate a deeper dive or a one-off session when it's genuinely needed, not every meeting by default.

Protect

Removes what's in the way

Surfaces and clears blockers, dependencies and organizational noise so the team can focus on the work.

THE MYTHS

Common misconceptions about Scrum Masters

The misconception
The reality
They're just a project manager with a new title
No assigning tasks or tracking deadlines — the team owns its commitments; the Scrum Master facilitates the process
They run the sprint
The team plans and runs the sprint — the Scrum Master removes blockers and keeps events useful, but doesn't direct the work
They're admin support for meetings
The real job is coaching the team on agile practices and protecting focus, not admin work
They report on the team to management
Policing velocity or reporting failures upward would undermine trust and turn the role into surveillance
They're only needed during the sprint
The job spans coaching, removing organisational obstacles, and improving team dynamics over time — well beyond the events
They make final decisions
Product decisions belong to the Product Owner; technical decisions belong to the developers — the Scrum Master has authority over neither
Anyone can do it as a side task
Facilitating well, coaching a team and spotting dysfunction take real skill — as a spare-time add-on, the role gets neglected

ADAPT
THE PRINCIPLE

The framework serves the team, not the other way around.

Scrum's own values ask us to inspect and adapt. Sticking rigidly to one framework's ceremonies, when the team's actual bottleneck sits elsewhere, works against that principle.

THE TOOLKIT

Borrowing what fits, from wherever it's from

XP

When quality is the bottleneck

Pairing, test-first development and a sustainable pace, brought in to raise engineering quality.

Lean

When handoffs are the bottleneck

Value-stream thinking and waste reduction, brought in to shorten the path from idea to done.

Kanban

When flow is the bottleneck

WIP limits, pull, and flow metrics, brought in to make delivery calmer and more predictable.

COACH
THE APPROACH

Scrum Master by title. Team Agile Coach by practice.

A framework-agnostic Scrum Master: same accountabilities to the team, a wider toolkit to draw on.

Fit over compliance
The right practice for this team, not the textbook one.
Inspect and adapt
Practices earn their place by working, not by being canon.
Coach first
Grows the team's own judgment, rather than owning it for them.

IN PRACTICE

What this looks like week to week

Events and artifacts get shaped around this team's actual bottleneck, not run because a guide says so.
Coaching conversations, not status chasing, are where most of the time goes.
Practices from XP, Lean or Kanban show up when the evidence points that way, never by default.
The team ends up owning its own process; the goal is to need this role less over time, not more.
